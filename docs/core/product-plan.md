# Hatchlane product plan

**First release · 22 November 2026 · Software simulation**

A field operator at a labelled manhole should be able to open its record on a phone and answer four questions. Is this the right access point? Which permit applies? What has actually been observed? Who owns the next action? A supervisor at a desk should see the same answers and be able to reconstruct the job after it ends.

Build for one customer and demonstrate at one site with two manholes, one drawing, named users and simulated equipment. Two manholes let the team test wrong-asset selection and simultaneous jobs. Keep records scoped to a site so another site can be added later. Use one responsive application and one site checklist. A small local setup is sufficient for the first release.

## The people and their workspace

| Person | Starting point | Actions |
|---|---|---|
| Site Admin | Site Explorer or **Review queue** in My permits | Set up the site and assets, manage site membership and duty eligibility, review submitted work and withdraw approval with a reason. A reviewer must be outside the assigned job team. |
| Field operator | Scan the asset label, type its ID or select a drawing pin | Confirm site and asset, draft a permit, record preparation and observations, request the appropriate release and complete physical closeout checks. |
| Designated entry supervisor | Assigned permit | Review current evidence, record or withhold formal entry authorization under the site procedure, stop work and sign off completion. |
| Designated attendant | Active permit on a phone | Record each person's entry and exit, see who is inside, flag changed conditions and reconcile the roster. Entrants can be listed by name without individual logins. |
| Simulator facilitator | **Simulator** from Site Explorer | Select the asset and permit, decide pending requests locally and submit independent simulated observations and faults. A separate named account holds this site permission. |

Admin and Operator are the two account roles. A Site Admin has the Admin role and membership in the site. Assign one field operator, one entry supervisor and one attendant as distinct eligible users on each permit. Each person signs their own decisions. Site members can browse equipment status, Admins can read the site's permits, and permit participants can read their assigned permits. The facilitator can see the permit number and equipment commands needed to operate the simulator. My permits brings each user back to jobs awaiting their action. Demo accounts stand in for site-qualified personnel. Keep the asset ID, permit number, site time, next action, responsible person and any blocking reason visible when a user returns to a job.

Users sign in with an Admin-provisioned email and password. Provisioning and reset issue a temporary password that the user changes before opening a workspace. A reset signs the user out of existing sessions. The Admin can disable access while keeping past decisions attributable to the original user. Sign-in and account recovery work on the site's network without an email service. [Account architecture](architecture.md#accounts-sessions-and-permissions)

In Site Explorer the drawing and searchable list share one record per manhole. Each shows the permit holding the reservation, including a future approved job, and flags open layers, unknown feedback and unresolved alarms. Selecting a manhole opens its two cover/bolt pairs, source times and current job. Both views update as events arrive, including changes on other assets at the site. Field users can reach the same detail by printed ID. A permit number links to the job for users with permission to read it.

## Permit lifecycle

| State | How it changes |
|---|---|
| **Draft** | The field operator saves edits and submits an immutable version. A return for changes reopens the draft with the review reason and leaves the submitted version in history. |
| **Awaiting review** | A Site Admin outside the team approves, returns or declines the submitted version. Approval requires a commissioned asset with no reservation, unresolved alarm or reconciliation hold, and an end time still in the future. |
| **Approved** | Holds the asset. The field operator confirms the physical label and starts the job during its window, with commissioning current and any alarm or reconciliation hold resolved. The window includes the start and ends at the specified end time. Use site time for entry and display. |
| **Active** | The field operator records preparations and requests release, the supervisor decides on entry authorization, and the attendant records movements. Outer access, assessment and attendance are progress within this state. |
| **Closeout** | The supervisor starts closeout on an active or stopped job. The attendant reconciles the roster, the operator attests to field checks and the supervisor closes the permit once the requirements are met. |
| **Declined, Cancelled, Closed** | Terminal results. Decline ends review. Verified unused cancellation and verified closure free the reservation. Authorized history readers can retrieve the record. |

An approved or active job can show **Stopped** with a reason. Keep that reason through closeout and preserve the reservation until verified cancellation or closure. A reconciliation hold can coexist with stopped work or closeout. Entry authorization, a request outcome, equipment observations and the roster remain visible as separate facts.

## The job from start to finish

### 1. Identify and commission the access point

The Admin creates the site and access point, uploads a drawing, and places one pin per manhole on the selected revision and page. Save its position relative to the page so it survives zoom and resize. Keep the previous revision available and ask the Admin to confirm the pin on a replacement drawing. Search and scan still work while a pin awaits review. Optional phone GPS, accuracy, capture time and photos help identify the installation. The pin is placed separately.

On site, scan or type each equipment serial. Assign one simulated gateway to the asset and one simulated device to each labelled layer, the outer solid cover and inner protective guard. Each layer device reports its position and its bolt separately. The Admin accepts commissioning after checking all three assignments and observing fresh secured feedback for each layer. Block duplicate device assignments. Replacement preserves prior assignments and requires a fresh check before use. During a job, replacement is available only through the stopped-job recovery described below. An asset QR opens the site-scoped asset record. A device QR identifies equipment during setup. Both require a signed-in user for changes.

### 2. Request and review work

From the asset, the field operator sees the current reservation and can draft a future job. Capture scope, requested start/end in site time, named team and duties, a named entrant roster, hazards, isolation measures, PPE, communications, rescue arrangements, a local emergency contact and checklist answers. Save and revisit an incomplete draft. Validate required fields, eligible duty assignments and the time window at submission. Preserve the submitted checklist and permit version.

The Admin opens the review queue, inspects the submitted version and approves, returns it with a specific change request, or declines with a reason. The operator sees the outcome in My permits. A return reopens the draft for resubmission. A decline ends that request. Approval reserves the commissioned asset for this job until closeout or unused cancellation. Multiple drafts can exist, but a second approval on that asset must show the blocking permit, even if two reviewers approve simultaneously. Future work can stay submitted. Display the opening time on an approved permit whose window has yet to start.

### 3. Prepare and request outer access

At the manhole, the assigned operator confirms the displayed site and asset ID against the physical label. An ID mismatch sends the operator back to asset lookup. Start the approved job within its window, record the pre-opening checks and required observations, then select **Request outer-lock release**. Confirm the target and reason. Request and local confirmation require fresh secured feedback for both layers and a current simulator connection. The facilitator acts as the independent local controller. Show the request result, the observed outer bolt position and the observed cover position separately. Advance to assessment after the acknowledged outer request and fresh observations of the bolt disengaged and the cover open.

### 4. Assess conditions and request inner access

The operator records atmosphere observations before opening when required, during assessment and throughout occupied work as the site procedure requires. Each record includes gases, values and units, sample time and location/depth, tester, instrument ID, calibration/bump-check status and source. The demo checklist supports oxygen, flammability, H₂S and CO. Mark manual observations as operator-recorded. Record ventilation equipment, planned duration, observed start/stop, interruptions and who confirmed operation. Record a reason when the procedure permits a step to be marked inapplicable.

The designated supervisor reviews the evidence, its age and instrument validity, isolations, rescue readiness and other required checks. Missing or invalid required evidence shows what the operator must supply. The supervisor records an explicit decision against the current permit and evidence. Withholding authorization leaves the operator able to add evidence for a new decision. **Request inner-guard release** requires current supervisor authorization, the assigned attendant online and responsible for the permit, and fresh feedback showing the outer cover open and its bolt disengaged, and the inner guard closed and its bolt engaged. The facilitator separately confirms or denies the request. Recheck these conditions at confirmation. A valid routine monitoring update continues the monitoring record. If required evidence expires, a correction invalidates the reviewed evidence, or a prohibited condition or alarm is reported after sign-off, invalidate authorization and pending requests and stop the job. Show the affected evidence and reason. Each layer has independent bolt and cover feedback. Local confirmation is required for every release.

Use one versioned demo checklist throughout the walkthrough. It defines the required pre-opening, assessment, ongoing monitoring and closeout fields, eligibility for **Inapplicable**, and the synthetic evidence outcomes used in the demos. Show the checklist version in history. [Simulation policy and timing](architecture.md#simulation-policy-and-timing)

### 5. Account for people and close the job

With current authorization, an acknowledged inner request and fresh open/disengaged feedback through both layers, the attendant selects a rostered person and records entry. Show names and times under **Currently inside**. Record each exit by name, allow re-entry under valid conditions, and prevent duplicate entry or exit for the same visit. Keep observations and stop-work actions within reach of the roster. Use **Record observed movement** to retain an unexpected person or movement even when planned entries are blocked. Flag the discrepancy and reconcile who is inside.

When work ends, the supervisor starts closeout. This blocks new release requests and planned entries and cancels pending requests. The attendant reconciles the roster and confirms everyone is out. A later observed entry reopens reconciliation and clears that confirmation. Confirm closure and manual bolt engagement in the simulator, inner guard first and then outer cover. If both layers are already secured, confirm them in the same order with fresh feedback. The operator records site closeout and return-to-service checks. The supervisor closes the permit when all four secured observations, the reconciled empty roster and current closeout attestations are present. An interrupted job can take this same path. If a check fails, retain the reservation and name the outstanding item and owner.

Closing frees the asset and preserves the permit record, accessible from the asset or permit search. Include the approved version, decisions, readings, ventilation, people movements, release requests, observations, issues and closeout. Show site time and preserve both observation and recording times. Corrections append the author, reason, original event reference and corrected value. Past decisions and sign-offs remain as recorded. The attendant or an eligible recovery attendant can report a movement after closure. Append the report to history and put the asset on a reconciliation hold when occupancy is uncertain. Preserve the completed permit and any later reservation. The Site Admin coordinates reconciliation and records its resolution.

## When work changes or stops

| Situation | What the user sees and can do |
|---|---|
| The plan changes after approval | Protect the reviewed scope, roster, checklist and time window. Cancel an unused job or stop and close out a started job. Copy the work details into a new draft for fresh review. Decisions and evidence stay with the original permit. |
| Work is stopped, approval is withdrawn or time runs out | The assigned operator, attendant or supervisor can stop work. The Site Admin can withdraw approval. Show the reason and who is inside. Block further release requests and planned entries, invalidate pending requests and retain exit logging, observations and closeout. The first build requires a new permit to restart work. Follow site emergency procedures where applicable. |
| An approved job never starts | The field operator or Site Admin can cancel it with a reason if it was never started, has no release or movements recorded, and fresh observations confirm both layers secured. Apply the same checks to an unused expired permit or approval withdrawal. Otherwise keep the reservation and complete field closeout. |
| Release denied, timed out or tapped twice | Keep one pending request per layer. A double tap yields the existing request. Show the outcome and wait for fresh feedback after the outcome before another attempt. An acknowledged request remains unresolved until its feedback confirms the open state or a stopped job enters recovery. Older feedback can appear in history without changing the current state. |
| A sensor is stale, contradictory or offline | Show the last observation time, source and affected layer. Mark current state unknown or in conflict. Hold actions that depend on it. An assigned operator records the issue and reconciles fresh feedback. Acknowledging an issue cannot satisfy a physical check. |
| A failed sensor needs replacement to finish closeout | Stop the job and reconcile everyone out. The Admin can then replace the simulated device, preserve both serials in history and repeat commissioning checks. Keep the asset reserved and the job stopped until fresh evidence and supervisor closeout. |
| An assigned duty holder becomes unavailable | Stop the job. A Site Admin records a reason and appoints an eligible replacement for a named recovery duty. Keep original assignments and signatures in history. The replacement can reconcile, record exits or complete closeout according to their duty. Continuing planned work needs a new permit. |
| A phone loses connection | Show connection loss, last contact and last confirmed save. Keep local accountability, emergency egress and rescue independent of the app. When connected, the attendant reconciles actual movements with event time, recording time and source before closeout or further entry. Release and entry authorization require deliberate online actions. |

Keep the authorization outcome visible through closeout. An expired job can still have two people inside and an open cover. Neither expiry, loss of communications nor a stop issues a relock. The simulator must discard unfulfilled requests on disconnect, expiry or invalidation and must never replay them on reconnect. The app records local activity and site decisions. Real equipment, local protective functions and emergency access require separate site engineering and acceptance.

## Build order and acceptance

Each slice includes the screens, saved records, permission checks and timeline events needed for its demonstration. Later slices extend the same asset and permit. All equipment observations and local decisions generated by the simulator carry a visible simulation label.

| Slice | What to deliver | Demonstrate before moving on |
|---|---|---|
| **1 · Find and review** | Seed a site, drawing and two commissioned simulated assets. Provision named accounts and build sign-in, drawing/list/ID lookup, asset detail, saved draft, review queue and decision history. | Sign in to the site on a phone, find MH-001, submit, receive a requested correction and resubmit. A different user approves. Refresh both sessions and see the same decision. A second permit cannot claim MH-001. MH-002 stays available. |
| **2 · Outer access** | Add the active permit view, pre-opening evidence, scoped requests, simulator controls, independent bolt/cover observations and server-enforced stop and expiry. | Acknowledge the request locally and withhold sensor feedback. The job still awaits physical confirmation. Send bolt and cover events separately. Repeat with denial, duplicate tap and lost connection. Stop a job with a pending request. Flag a fault on MH-002 while MH-001 is selected, then open the affected record from Explorer. |
| **3 · Authorization and attendance** | Add assessment, ventilation log, supervisor decision, inner release, attendant roster and reconnect reconciliation for continued attendance. | A completed timer leaves the supervisor's step pending. With one required reading missing, the supervisor sees what is needed. Supply it, authorize, observe inner opening and record two entrants. One exit leaves one named person inside. Let required evidence expire and verify that exits remain available while new entries are blocked. |
| **4 · Closeout and recovery** | Add withdrawal and cancellation, recovery assignments, closeout reconciliation and verification, searchable history and backup recovery. | Expire a job with one person inside. Record exit. Withhold outer-bolt feedback and confirm closeout stays blocked. Restore fresh observations, close, retrieve the full job and approve the next one. On another run, replace the unavailable attendant, record an unexpected entry during closeout and verify the all-out confirmation clears. Also cancel an unused job. |
| **5 · Commissioning** | Admin creates a site asset, uploads/revises a drawing, places its pin, pairs simulated devices, verifies feedback and accepts commissioning. Add field QR scan and optional GPS/photo capture. | Add MH-003 through the UI. Deny location permission and use the printed ID. Reject an already assigned serial. Revise the drawing and confirm the pin. Have the team run a permit on MH-003. Fail a sensor during closeout and recover by replacing and checking it. |

**First-release acceptance.** A desk reviewer, field operator, entry supervisor, attendant and simulator facilitator can complete the five demonstrations using their normal screens. Use a fresh, explicitly seeded demo instance for independent runs that end with a reservation. The permit timeline explains who decided, what equipment reported, who was inside and what the job needed to close.

**Later work.** After the software walkthrough, test one controller and two locks on a nonhazardous bench. The first field pilot depends on the site's approved procedure, qualified role assignments, applicable legal and equipment review, and validated independent controls, egress and rescue arrangements. Vendor gas telemetry, multi-site rollout, enterprise sign-in, fleet maintenance and deployment hardening can follow separately. Before bench work, confirm available position signals and local request outcomes with the hardware provider. Before field use, have the site owner define required evidence, validity windows and sign-offs.

The design system supplies screen terminology and visual conventions. For a reference example of named entrant accountability, see the attendant duties in paragraph (i)(3) of the U.S. standard. The site owner determines which requirements apply locally.
